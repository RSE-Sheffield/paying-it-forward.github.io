Paying It Forward

How are research outputs changing in the age of AI?

Paying It Forward aims to build a metric, or set of criteria, that describes whether a piece of research generates new research artefacts, and what kind of artefacts it produces.

We limit the scope to software and data. There are many other kinds of research artefact, such as new theoretical methodologies, hardware or protocols, but these two are where we start.

The taxonomy

Each paper is rated on two axes, giving nine categories from S1D1 to S3D3.

Generated (1)
New or substantially extended software or data that plays a substantial role in the research and is shared or published.
Used (2)
Existing software or data that plays a substantial role, but is not substantially developed by the research team.
None (3)
Software or data does not play a substantial role in achieving the research goals.

For example, a project that generates new software and uses existing data is S1D2.

Approach

We are testing whether large language models, both local and via API, can classify papers into these categories reliably, and how their judgements compare with a manual pass by people.
