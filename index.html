Paying It Forward

How are research outputs changing in the age of AI?

Explore the nine categories View on GitHub

OverviewA metric for classifying research outputs

AI is accelerating research productivity, particularly in data-rich analysis and automated software generation, potentially shifting incentives away from creating new research resources. Yet there is currently no systematic way to measure how research communities produce, use, or extend essential artefacts such as data and software.

Paying It Forward builds a metric, or set of criteria, that describes whether a piece of research generates new research artefacts, and what kind of artefacts it produces.

We limit the scope to software and data. Arguably, there are many other types of research artefact, such as a new theoretical methodology, hardware or protocols.

What do we mean by data, software and “other”?
Definition Typical evidence in a paper
Research Data
“Research data can be defined as ‘recorded factual material commonly retained by and accepted in the scientific community as necessary to validate research findings’. Although the majority of such data is created in digital format, all research data is included in the definition, irrespective of the format in which it is created.” – UKRI, 2026 (source)
A collection of observations or information produced, collected, processed or released by the research. 		Dataset, corpus, database, annotations, benchmark data, supplementary data, model weights, lab books
Research Software
“Research Software includes source code files, algorithms, scripts, computational workflows and executables that were created during the research process or for a research purpose. Software Components (e.g. operating systems, libraries, dependencies, packages, scripts, etc.) that are used for research but were not created during or with a clear research intent should be considered software in research and not Research Software.” (10.5281/zenodo.5504015)
An executable computational artefact produced or used as part of the research. 		Source code, package, scripts, application, model implementation
Other Research artefacts that are neither data nor software. Hardware, physical materials, protocols, instruments, figures, documentation, experimental designs, or simply no research artefacts produced outside of the paper, such as a literature review
The scale: generated, used, or not relevant
Substantial role: the software or data is materially involved in producing, analysing, validating, or enabling the research results, rather than being used only for incidental, administrative, or routine purposes.

Software

S1

Research Software Generated

New or substantially extended software that plays a substantial role in achieving the research goals, or is itself a research goal. It should not be a utility but rather represent substantial research effort. The paper should describe how the research created or significantly extended research software. Crucially, the software created should generate new knowledge.

S2

Research Software Used

Existing software that plays a substantial role in achieving the research goals, but is not substantially generated or extended by the research team. It should not be a utility but rather represent substantial research effort. The paper should describe how the research uses existing research software, and the software used should generate new knowledge.

S3

No Research Software

Either software is not used, or the software used does not create new knowledge but instead acts as a utility, or does not play a substantial role in achieving the research goals.

Data

D1

Research Data Generated

New or substantially extended data that plays a substantial role in achieving the research goals, or is itself a research goal. The paper should describe how the research created or significantly extended research data. Crucially, the data created should generate new knowledge.

D2

Research Data Used

Existing data that plays a substantial role in achieving the research goals, but is not substantially generated or extended by the research team. The paper should describe how the research used existing research data, and the data used should generate new knowledge.

D3

No Research Data

Either research data is not used, or the data used does not create new knowledge. Data does not play a substantial role in achieving the research goals.

Some general rules

Generated implies used

If a paper both uses and generates software or data, it belongs in the generated category. We assume that anything generated is also used.

When unclear, go higher

If the right category is unclear, err towards the higher number. Generated (1) needs clear evidence that the research produced it; otherwise classify it as used (2).

The paper is the ground truth

If a paper says it generated software or data but gives no way to access it, we still count it as S1 or D1. Future work packages will assess data stewardship and FAIRness.

Researchers conduct a survey of 5,000 participants and create and release an anonymised dataset of survey responses. The dataset is a substantial research output. Literature review of global warming

A review of existing work that produces no research artefacts outside of the paper itself.

Position or opinion paper, or theoretical algorithm

Argument or theory is the contribution; no software or data plays a substantial role.

Experimental protocol (e.g. for medical trials)

Details of an experimental procedure published before any investigation is carried out. The protocol sets out what data will be collected and what software will be written to analyse the data, but neither are contributed in this paper.

EvaluationHow do we evaluate this metric?

This metric can be evaluated by taking a research paper and using the criteria to give it a classification (e.g. S2D3). For scalability, we are testing whether large language models, both local and frontier models, can classify papers into these categories reliably, and how their judgements compare with human classifications. Once we have established a reliable method, we can apply it to a larger dataset to gain insights into the current state of research output.
